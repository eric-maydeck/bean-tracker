Kinini Washing Station

Located in the Northern Province, Kinini Washing Station serves 48 members from Tumba village, with 85% being women, demonstrating a strong commitment to gender equality in the coffee industry. Launched in 2014, Kinini Washing Station signed “lease” agreements with local farmers, committing to provide seedlings, training, and organizational support. In return, farmers only needed to commit to delivering cherries to Kinini washing station.

Kinini Washing Station is more than a conventional business project, it’s a comprehensive community development model. 10% of profits are directly invested in education and healthcare development projects.

Recommendations for storage and use

– Coffee wants to rest in a cool place away from sunlight and sealed bags;
– Our coffee reaches its peak flavor 04 weeks after roasting for beans that are not packaged and are served directly at our store chain. For packaged coffee intended for distribution, we apply flavor-locking technology. As a natural result, it may take 8 – 12 weeks, or even up to 16 weeks after roasting, for the coffee to reach its peak flavor (Please note that the resting time required to reach peak flavor is directly proportional to the amount of time the coffee remains sealed in flavor-locking packaging. The longer the coffee stays in an airtight bag, the longer it will take to degas and reach its optimal peak).
– Our coffee maintains its aroma spectrum within 06 months of opening the bag, and the quality gradually decreases;
– Our coffee is packed with flavor-locking technology, so it can be preserved for 02 years from the date of production when unopened. We also recommend not using it when first opening the bag. Please open and close the bag for another 03 days to fully release the flavor-locking encapsulation technology;
– Our coffee will clearly show the flavor description after 03 days from when you open and close the bag for the first time.

El Limoncillo Yellow Pacamara was quite unusual. Sweet, rich, earthy and herbal notes dominated initially. As the cup cooled, some dank, juicy, exotic tropical fruit notes appeared. Kiwi or starfruit perhaps. Overripe mango was noticeable too. Rainbow Skittles also came to mind. The herbal notes reappeared as the coffee cooled and competed with the tropical fruits for dominance. Some roastiness was also detectable. The flavor profile was also dense, earthy and quite savory but lacked the sweetness of the aroma. There was a crisp, lemony brightness up front before the big, savory, woody notes appeared. Some exotic tropical fruits were noticeable as the coffee moved along the palate, but they were overshadowed by the earthiness. Starfruit perhaps. Bit of dank, overripe mango too. There was more crisp, zippy, lemony acidity in the center. A hint of roast was noticeable on the back end. The finish was earthy and dense with a lingering herbality. All in all, medium-high sweetness in the aroma and medium-low sweetness in the flavor. Medium high acidity in the aroma and exceptionally high acidity in the flavor. The mouthfeel was medium-full bodied but oddly chalky and drying. Pacamaras are possibly my least favorite varietal of coffee, and this offering was a reminder as to why
